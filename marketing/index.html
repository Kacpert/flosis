Why this is expensive

A vague ticket doesn't stay a ticket problem for long.

It becomes a code problem, then a review problem, then a bug with no clear owner. Every tool you've bought sits downstream of the moment the damage was done.

It starts upstream

GitLab's DevSecOps survey puts unclear requirements ahead of technical complexity and resource constraints as the top cause of delays.

Your agents inherit it

Autonomous coding agents need precise acceptance criteria — vague tickets produce vague pull requests. Spec quality caps everything downstream.

Generators made it faster, not clearer

A well-formatted ticket with no context is a conversation starter, not a specification. The back-and-forth just moves to the pull request.

And you can't see the cost

Re-work rarely gets logged as re-work. It shows up as a slower sprint, a mystery bug, and a number nobody can explain to finance.

The pipeline

From "we should probably…" to a branch a developer can open.

Five stages, every state mirrored in Jira, and a human approving each handoff. Nothing here is a black box — you decide when a brief is good, when a spec ships, and when code merges.

1

Capture

A new idea, or an existing Jira ticket from any board. Ideas are saved and titled so nothing gets lost between refinements.

2

Briefing

The AI acts like a Product Owner — probing for user value, proposing the best fit for the existing app, challenging requirements only when it genuinely matters.

3

Designs

Optional. Request Figma work from the designer, or skip it — the flow never stalls on tasks that don't need visuals.

4

Details Gathering

Grounded in the code and the designs. Product questions only, one at a time — it looks up the technical answers itself.

5

Estimate & pre-code

Story points into your own Jira field, then a branch with the spec and scaffold already on it.

Every version is kept

Your own description is v0. Each AI draft and manual edit becomes a new version, and you choose which is current — that's the one written back to Jira.

Local until you say so

Run the whole process on an idea that isn't in Jira yet. Push it when it's ready and the ticket is created with the final description in place.

Skip what you don't need

Already know the why? Start at Details Gathering. Need designs after the spec instead of before? Also fine. The pipeline bends to the task.

Pre-coding

The ticket arrives with a branch already waiting.

Once a task is detailed and estimated, Flosis opens a dedicated branch and does the groundwork: the spec committed next to the code, the files the change actually touches stubbed out, and pending tests named after the acceptance criteria.

No blank-page start. The developer opens the branch and the shape of the change is already there.
The spec travels with the code. No tab-switching to remember what "done" means.
Nothing merges for you. Flosis pushes branches and comments on PRs. It never approves and never merges — a human always does.

Ruby on SaaS is a software house. We spend our days inside other people's codebases, and the pattern never changed: the ticket said one thing, the code said another, and somebody lost two days finding out which.

We tried the obvious fixes. Better templates didn't help — people filled them in. Faster ticket generators didn't help either; they just produced tidier guesses. That's Flosis. We use it on our own client work every week, which is also why it refuses to approve or merge anything — we wouldn't trust a tool that did, and neither should you.

Questions

Before you get in touch

Do we have to leave Jira?

No. Flosis sits on top of Jira Cloud and GitHub, reading boards and writing to fields you control — including its own AI estimation and AI actions fields. Your team keeps working where it already works.

Will it merge something we didn't review?

Never. Flosis comments on pull requests and pushes branches. Approving and merging are human actions, always — the same applies to briefs, specs and anything written back to Jira.

How does it know our codebase?

Your repository is checked out server-side with a token you provide, and master is refreshed daily. The AI reads whatever files it needs before asking a question, alongside a short architecture description you write once.

What if the AI writes something wrong?

You edit it. Every brief and spec is versioned — your own text is v0, each draft or edit adds a version, and only the one you mark current reaches Jira. You can reset the conversation and keep every version.

Does every ticket need designs?

No. Designs are an optional step you can request from your designer, skip entirely, or add after the spec instead of before it. Requested and ready states show on the ticket so nobody has to chase.

Can we run more than one project?

Yes — each project has its own repository, boards, credentials, agents and reporting, and switching projects swaps all of it at once. Pricing is per project, so you add them as you need them.

Bring us your vaguest ticket.

Half an hour on your own repository, on the worst-written thing in your backlog. You'll know by the end whether the questions it asks are the ones your team keeps having — and that's a fair way to judge it.
